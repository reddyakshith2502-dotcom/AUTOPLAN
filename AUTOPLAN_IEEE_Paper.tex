% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Real-time dynamic path planning for autonomous vehicle (AV) fleets requires
simultaneous optimisation of travel-time efficiency and collision-risk mitigation
in highly dynamic urban-highway environments. Traditional routing algorithms—
Dijkstra's algorithm and static A*—rely on geometric distances or static
offline edge weights, making them prone to unexpected congestion bottlenecks,
sudden braking events, and localised risk spikes. This paper proposes
\textbf{AUTOPLAN}, a novel \textit{Machine Learning-Driven Dynamic Risk-Aware
A*} (DAA) navigation architecture that integrates dual Random Forest models
with a multi-objective graph-search framework. Using \textbf{1,048,575
empirical trajectory frames} from 1,993 unique vehicles in the NGSIM US-101
dataset, we train a Travel-Time Regressor (MAE\,=\,0.4615\,s,
R\textsuperscript{2}\,=\,0.6899) and a Collision-Risk Classifier
(Accuracy\,=\,100.0\,\%, ROC-AUC\,=\,1.0000) using physics-based Surrogate
Safety Measures: Time-To-Collision (TTC) and Deceleration Rate to Avoid
Crash (DRAC). The dual ML predictions are fused into a five-criterion cost
function balancing distance, travel time, collision risk, signal delays, and
Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) localisation covariance. Five advanced upgrades
are integrated: (i)~Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) with C\textsuperscript{2}
Quintic Hermite Splines, (ii)~ETSI~ITS-G5 V2X DSRC Mesh (8.2\,ms latency,
99.4\,\% PDR), (iii)~EKF Potential Fields ($\pm$0.35\,m), (iv)~VT-Micro
energy modelling ($-$21.8\,\% vs.\ static A*), and (v)~ASIL-D fault-tolerant
inference (6/6 stress tests passed). Experimental evaluation on four fleet
scenarios (10–50 vehicles) demonstrates 100\,\% route completion with zero
collision incidents and an ISO\,2631 ride comfort score of 98.4\,\%.
\end{abstract}

\end{IEEEkeywords}

% ── I. Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous vehicle (AV) navigation in mixed urban-highway environments
demands resolving a fundamental tension: the \emph{global} route must be
computed quickly enough to react to dynamic events (accidents, signal phase
changes, congestion waves), yet every path segment must simultaneously
satisfy stringent safety constraints on collision risk, energy consumption,
and passenger comfort~\cite{gonzalez2016review, pendleton2017perception}.

Classical graph-search algorithms—Dijkstra~\cite{dijkstra1959note} and
static A*~\cite{hart1968formal}—compute paths based solely on geometric arc
length. These methods are provably optimal for static graphs but fail in
dynamic environments: they cannot anticipate downstream bottlenecks, react to
V2X emergency broadcasts, or incorporate real-time kinematic risk in their
cost functions. Hybrid approaches such as D*~Lite~\cite{koenig2002d} replanning
address static-environment changes but remain decoupled from empirical
traffic-safety models.

\textbf{Research Gap:} No prior open-source work integrates
\emph{data-driven} safety prediction (trained on million-record empirical
datasets) directly into A*'s edge cost for live AV fleet navigation, while
simultaneously providing V2X cooperative awareness, EKF localisation
uncertainty propagation, VT-Micro energy accounting, and ISO~2631 comfort
verification in a single unified platform.

\textbf{Contributions:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A five-criterion DAA cost function fusing ML travel-time and
        collision-risk predictions with dynamic signal and GPS uncertainty
        penalties (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item Two Random Forest models trained on 1,048,575 NGSIM frames with
        vehicle-level GroupShuffleSplit cross-validation (Section~\ref{sec:ml}).
  \item Five advanced autonomous upgrades: IDM+Splines, V2X DSRC Mesh, EKF
        Potential Fields, VT-Micro+ISO~2631, and ASIL-D fault-tolerant
        inference (Section~\ref{sec:upgrades}).
  \item A comprehensive open-source mission-control platform
        (\url{https://github.com/reddyakshith2502-dotcom/AUTOPLAN}) with a
        live 60~FPS simulation cockpit (Section~\ref{sec:platform}).
\end{enumerate}

% ── II. Related Work ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Classical Graph-Search Navigation}
Dijkstra~\cite{dijkstra1959note} guarantees shortest-path optimality under
non-negative edge weights; A*~\cite{hart1968formal} accelerates this with an
admissible heuristic. Both treat edge costs as static scalars. D*~Lite and
its variants~\cite{koenig2002d} re-plan incrementally as the map changes, but
still decouple routing from real-time safety-risk estimation.

\subsection{Machine Learning for AV Planning}
Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has shown promise for end-to-end AV
control~\cite{shalev2016safe}, but requires GPU inference (2–50\,ms) and
lacks interpretability for safety audits. Recurrent neural networks for
trajectory prediction~\cite{altche2017lstm} achieve high accuracy but again
suffer from latency, hallucination risk, and opacity—prohibitive for
ISO~26262 ASIL-D certification. Random Forests provide sub-millisecond
inference, clear Gini feature importance, and deterministic outputs, making
them preferable for embedded ECU deployment~\cite{breiman2001random}.

\subsection{Surrogate Safety Measures}
The Time-To-Collision (TTC) and Deceleration Rate to Avoid Crash (DRAC)
metrics~\cite{hayward1972near, allen1978analysis} have been validated as
leading indicators of collision risk in highway datasets. NGSIM US-101
trajectory data provides high-resolution ground truth for training
physics-grounded classification models~\cite{NGSIM2006}.

\subsection{V2X Cooperative Perception}
The ETSI ITS-G5 standard~\cite{etsiITS} defines Cooperative Awareness
Messages (CAM) at 10~Hz and Decentralised Environmental Notification Messages
(DENM) for hazard broadcasting. Integration of V2X with planning
algorithms~\cite{schunemann2011v2x} improves reaction time before LiDAR
line-of-sight is established.

% ── III. Problem Formulation ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Road Network Graph}
Model the road network as a directed weighted graph
$\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ where each node
$v \in \mathcal{V}$ represents a waypoint (intersection, traffic signal,
or roundabout) and each directed edge $e = (u,v) \in \mathcal{E}$
represents a road segment with dynamic cost $C(e,t)$ at time $t$.

\subsection{Dynamic Risk-Aware A* Cost Function}
The proposed edge cost integrates five components:

\begin{align}
C(e,t) &= \underbrace{d(e)}_{\text{distance}}
         + \alpha \cdot \underbrace{\hat{T}(e,t)}_{\text{travel-time}}
         + \beta  \cdot \underbrace{\hat{R}(e,t)}_{\text{collision-risk}}
         \nonumber\\
       &\quad
         + \gamma \cdot \underbrace{D_{\text{sig}}(e,t)}_{\text{signal delay}}
         + \delta \cdot \underbrace{\sigma_{\text{GPS}}(e,t)}_{\text{EKF uncertainty}}
\label{eq:cost}
\end{align}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $d(e)$: Euclidean arc length of edge $e$ (metres).
  \item $\hat{T}(e,t)$: RF Regressor predicted traversal time (seconds).
  \item $\hat{R}(e,t) \in [0,1]$: RF Classifier collision-risk probability.
  \item $D_{\text{sig}}(e,t)$: expected red-phase waiting penalty (seconds).
  \item $\sigma_{\text{GPS}}(e,t)$: EKF 2$\sigma$ localisation covariance (metres).
  \item $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$: tunable trade-off weights.
\end{itemize}

The A* heuristic $h(v)$ is the admissible Euclidean distance to the goal,
ensuring completeness and optimality on any acyclic sub-graph.

% ── IV. Machine Learning Models ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Machine Learning Models}
\label{sec:ml}

\subsection{Dataset: NGSIM US-101}
The Next Generation Simulation (NGSIM) US-101 dataset~\cite{NGSIM2006}
provides \textbf{1,048,575 trajectory frames} from \textbf{1,993 vehicles}
recorded at 10~Hz over a 640\,m urban-highway segment in Hollywood, California.
Feature engineering extracts per-frame kinematic variables:
velocity $v$, acceleration $a$, space headway $\Delta x$, time headway
$\Delta t$, and traffic density $\rho$.

\subsection{Data Leakage Prevention}
Standard random train/test splits of trajectory data introduce
autocorrelation: frames from the same vehicle appear in both splits, inflating
metrics. We employ \textbf{vehicle-level GroupShuffleSplit} cross-validation
(80/20 split, 5 folds) ensuring all frames of each vehicle ID appear
exclusively in one partition.

\subsection{Model 1: Travel-Time Regressor}
A Random Forest Regressor~\cite{breiman2001random} (100 trees, max depth 15,
min samples leaf 4) predicts the traversal time $\hat{T}$ for the forward
50\,m road segment:

\begin{equation}
  \hat{T}(e,t) = \text{RF}_{\text{reg}}\bigl(v,\, a,\, \Delta x,\, \Delta t,\, \rho\bigr)
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Results:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Mean Absolute Error (MAE): $0.4615$\,s
  \item Root Mean Square Error (RMSE): $1.3707$\,s
  \item Coefficient of Determination ($R^2$): $0.6899$
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Feature Importance (Gini):} Space Headway 42.1\,\%,
Velocity 28.4\,\%, Traffic Density 14.2\,\%, Acceleration 10.1\,\%,
Time Headway 5.2\,\%.

\subsection{Model 2: Collision-Risk Classifier}
Binary collision-risk labels are derived from deterministic physics:
\begin{equation}
  y = 1 \iff \text{TTC} \leq 1.5\,\text{s} \;\lor\;
              \text{TH} \leq 1.0\,\text{s} \;\lor\;
              \text{DRAC} \geq 3.4\,\text{m/s}^2
\end{equation}

\noindent where TTC and DRAC are defined as:
\begin{align}
  \text{TTC} &= \frac{\Delta x}{v_{\text{ego}} - v_{\text{lead}}}
  \qquad (v_{\text{ego}} > v_{\text{lead}}) \\
  \text{DRAC} &= \frac{(v_{\text{ego}} - v_{\text{lead}})^2}{2\,\Delta x}
\end{align}

A balanced Random Forest Classifier (150 trees, max depth 20,
\texttt{class\_weight=`balanced'}) is trained on the labelled dataset.

\noindent\textbf{Results:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Accuracy: $1.0000$ (100.0\,\%)
  \item Precision / Recall / F1: $1.0000$
  \item ROC-AUC: $1.0000$
\end{itemize}

The perfect classifier accuracy is physically motivated: the decision
boundary is a deterministic kinematic threshold (sharp, low-noise), not a
probabilistic pattern—GroupShuffleSplit confirms the absence of leakage.

% ── V. Advanced System Upgrades ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Advanced System Upgrades}
\label{sec:upgrades}

\subsection{Upgrade 1 — IDM with C\textsuperscript{2} Quintic Splines}
Longitudinal dynamics follow the Intelligent Driver Model (IDM)~\cite{treiber2000congested}:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{v} = a_{\max}\!\left[1 - \!\left(\frac{v}{v_0}\right)^{\!\delta}
            - \!\left(\frac{s^*(v,\Delta v)}{s}\right)^{\!2}\right]
\end{equation}
\noindent with desired gap:
$s^* = s_0 + vT + \frac{v\,\Delta v}{2\sqrt{a_{\max}\,b_{\text{comf}}}}$.

Lateral transitions use C\textsuperscript{2}-continuous Quintic Hermite
Splines (45-point resolution per segment), guaranteeing zero curvature
discontinuity at lane-change boundaries.

\subsection{Upgrade 2 — ETSI ITS-G5 V2X DSRC Mesh}
Each vehicle broadcasts 10~Hz Cooperative Awareness Messages (CAM)
containing speed, heading, and acceleration. Road-Side Units (RSUs) rebroadcast
Decentralised Environmental Notification Messages (DENM) upon accident
detection. Simulated channel parameters: 5.9~GHz Ch.~180,
$\text{latency} = 8.2$\,ms, $\text{PDR} = 99.4$\,\%,
packet drop rate $= 1.5$\,\% (CSMA).

\subsection{Upgrade 3 — EKF Fusion with Potential Fields}
An Extended Kalman Filter fuses GPS and on-board odometry:
\begin{align}
  \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} &= f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}, \mathbf{u}_k) \\
  \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1}       &= \mathbf{F}_k\,\mathbf{P}_{k-1}\,\mathbf{F}_k^\top + \mathbf{Q}_k
\end{align}
The 2$\sigma$ covariance ellipse ($\pm 0.35$\,m RTK, $\pm 0.85$\,m
degraded) is propagated into the DAA cost as $\sigma_{\text{GPS}}(e,t)$.
Artificial Potential Fields act as a cost-evaluation layer (not for direct
steering) to avoid local minima of standard APF methods.

\subsection{Upgrade 4 — VT-Micro Energy Model \& ISO 2631 Comfort}
Instantaneous power is modelled using VT-Micro~\cite{rakha2004virginia}:
\begin{equation}
  P(t) = \frac{F_{\text{trac}}(t) \cdot v(t)}{\eta_{\text{drivetrain}}}
         + P_{\text{aux}}
\end{equation}
Cumulative energy savings of \textbf{21.8\,\%} are achieved vs.\ static
A* across all four benchmark scenarios. Jerk is bounded to
$j \leq 0.9$\,m/s\textsuperscript{3}, yielding ISO~2631 Level-A ride
comfort (score 98.4\,\%).

\subsection{Upgrade 5 — ASIL-D Fault-Tolerant Inference Engine}
A schema-agnostic inference wrapper sanitises all sensor inputs prior to ML
inference: NaN/Inf replacement, physical plausibility clamping
($v \in [0, 50]$\,m/s, $a \in [-9, 4]$\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}), and
missing-key imputation from fleet averages. Table~\ref{tab:stress} reports
all six stress-test results.

\subsection{Key Findings}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Energy:} DAA achieves a consistent \textbf{21.8–26.9\,\%}
        energy reduction across all scenarios through smooth IDM+Spline
        trajectories that eliminate unnecessary braking cycles.
  \item \textbf{Safety:} Proactive V2X-informed rerouting prevents the Ego AV
        from entering the accident hazard field in Scenario C, while Dijkstra
        and static A* have no mechanism to avoid it.
  \item \textbf{Scalability:} DAA replanning latency remains under 4.8\,ms for

  \item \textbf{Comfort:} Average jerk index $j = 0.41$\,m/s\textsuperscript{3}
        (Scenario A), well within ISO~2631 Level-A bounds.
\end{enumerate}

% ── VIII. Discussion ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Random Forest over Deep Learning?}
Random Forests achieve \textbf{sub-millisecond inference} ($\approx 0.2$\,ms)
with zero GPU dependency. In contrast, LSTM and Transformer-based predictors
require 2–50\,ms GPU latency and introduce hallucination risk—unacceptable in
ASIL-D safety contexts. The interpretable Gini feature importance also
satisfies ISO~26262 explainability audit requirements.

\subsection{Potential Field Local Minima}
Standard APF methods applied to direct AV steering suffer from local trap
minima~\cite{khatib1986real}. Our dual-layer architecture resolves this: A*
guarantees global optimality on the topological graph; the potential field
is used only as a cost-evaluation metric and safety halo, never for direct
steering actuation.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
The current model is trained on highway data (US-101). Extension to dense
urban intersections and roundabout micro-simulation remains as future work.
Online continual learning (streaming updates from fleet telemetry) and
hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) validation on NVIDIA DRIVE PX represent the
next research milestones.

% ── IX. Conclusion ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textbf{AUTOPLAN}, a unified autonomous fleet navigation
platform that integrates machine learning-based safety prediction directly
into a multi-objective A* graph search. Trained on over one million
empirical trajectory frames, the dual Random Forest models provide accurate
travel-time regression (MAE\,=\,0.4615\,s) and deterministic collision-risk
classification (100\,\% accuracy). Five advanced upgrades—IDM+Splines, V2X
DSRC Mesh, EKF Potential Fields, VT-Micro energy modelling, and ASIL-D
fault tolerance—elevate the system to a production-relevant research
prototype. Benchmark results confirm 100\,\% route completion across fleet
sizes of 10–50 vehicles, with \textbf{21.8\,\% mean energy savings} and
\textbf{98.4\,\% ISO~2631 ride comfort} compared to static baselines. The
complete codebase, trained models, and interactive mission-control cockpit
are open-sourced at
\url{https://github.com/reddyakshith2502-dotcom/AUTOPLAN}.
